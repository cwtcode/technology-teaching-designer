# Lesson planning and topic/demo suggestions

## Lesson plan schema

Include title, subject, jurisdiction where relevant, form/year and readiness, language, duration, resources, setup, prerequisites, diagnostic check, vocabulary, objectives, and success criteria.

Read lesson-plan-format.md and use its saved two-table school layout as the default presentation. Use the internal planning fields below to develop the lesson, then map them into the visible four-column outline:
Stage/time | objective | teacher action and key questions | student action and decision | resource/task reference | evidence/check | support/response.
Do not expose these seven internal fields as a replacement table. Present the outline as Time | Content | Activity | Remarks, retaining explicit objectives, platform, task outputs and assessment evidence in the appropriate cells or concise teacher notes.

Follow the five-stage default in SKILL.md. State realistic expected responses and alternatives. Include saving/submission instructions and an offline or low-tech fallback. Put theory rationale and anticipated difficulties in concise teacher-facing sections. Include separate core, supported, and extension pathways without fixed learner labels.

Make every objective observable. Replace "understand loops" with a suitable expectation such as "predict a loop's output, explain its stopping condition, and adapt it for a new case." Include conceptual learning alongside operating procedures. Do not turn all learning into a checklist of clicks.

When inquiry is relevant, include an investigable question and planned pauses. Let students propose test cases or approaches. Preserve enough modelling for novices. Use peer/teacher feedback with time for action. Keep conclusion questions individual even after group work.

## Platform and assessed task specification

Include the following platform and task specifications in each lesson plan, even when the teacher has not asked separately. Embed them in the saved format’s Activity and Remarks cells, with concise teacher notes after the outline if necessary; separate report-style sections are not required.

**Recommended platform:** name a concrete platform/environment, its purpose in the lesson, why it fits the learners and objectives, required devices/software/connectivity, setup before class, account/access requirements, saving/submission method, and a feasible alternative or offline route. Prefer a manageable recommendation over a long list. If a tool is specified by the teacher, keep it where workable and explain any constraint. Verify current product details from official sources when browsing is available; do not invent free tiers, age permissions, features, or interface steps. Do not switch platforms unnecessarily between lessons.

**Task to finish within this lesson:** provide a concise task card:
- Task ID and student-facing brief.
- Platform and prepared starter/resources.
- Learning objective IDs assessed.
- Minimum completed product or response; distinguish essential requirements from extensions.
- Work time, testing/feedback/revision time, and assessment/collection time within the lesson budget.
- Observable success criteria or a small criterion checklist, with expected outcomes or exemplar reasoning.
- Evidence to collect, including an individual check for paired/group or AI-assisted work.
- Supported route, technical fallback, and next action for each unmet criterion.

Use the task output and its explanation/tests to assess learning. A completed product alone does not demonstrate every objective. Include additional short evidence where needed, and reduce objectives if they cannot be assessed meaningfully in time. Use criterion-level judgements such as demonstrated / developing / not yet evidenced; do not infer achievement from submission alone.

For example: in a 40-minute S2 selection lesson using a preconfigured block-programming editor, complete a reward checker. It must choose the correct response below, at, and above a specified threshold. Collect the working blocks or a paper equivalent, three expected/actual test results, and one individual boundary explanation. Completion requires the checker and tests; understanding requires explaining why equality takes the chosen branch. Prepare setup before class and reduce the task to arranging/tracing blocks if necessary. Treat this as an illustrative design, not a tested classroom outcome or a current product capability claim.

## Topic and demonstration selection

Offer about three distinct viable options unless requested otherwise. Compare learning purpose, prerequisites, stage, estimated time, tool/setup, demo outline, inquiry question, meaningful student choices, probable difficulties, assessment evidence, and extension. Recommend the best fit with a reason grounded in context. For each option, name a suitable platform and a realistic in-lesson core task, its completed output, objective-linked criteria, and assessment evidence.

Choose relatable, inclusive contexts and manageable scope. A visually attractive demo should still expose the intended concept. Prepare expected outputs, a deliberately faulty or contrasting example when useful, and a fallback. Verify current APIs and features before giving step-by-step interface instructions.

Avoid human BMI classification or medical interpretations in child-facing demonstrations; use synthetic inputs and focus on computation if such a context is requested. For science-linked examples, check the domain facts and avoid implying a simulated tool is a real measuring instrument.

## AI lesson framework alignment

Read ai-framework-alignment.md before planning AI learning. Add a compact matrix to Teacher notes: objective ID | OECD/EU domain, competence and level | UNESCO student aspect, competence and level | task/activity | evidence and success criterion. Cite edition and verified source locator. Include a realistic in-lesson task and individual evidence. Keep the visible outline Time | Content | Activity | Remarks; use Remarks to signpost the mapping and assessment. Do not equate use of AI-generated code with designing an AI system.

## Information and media literacy lessons

Read information-and-media-literacy.md and identify embedded or standalone mode. In embedded mode, show the literacy objective, activity and evidence alongside the host-subject learning, with time explicitly allocated. In standalone mode, use the five-stage sequence with source/media analysis demonstrations rather than forcing a programming demo. Recommend a suitable research/annotation/presentation environment or a prepared paper source pack. Require a finished evaluation, annotated media item, source record, or justified communication decision with clear criteria.

## Example invocation patterns

- "Create a 40-minute S2 lesson plan on Python input validation, with peer feedback and an independent task."
- "Suggest three block-based programming demos for beginner primary students with shared tablets."
- "Improve this technology lesson using guided inquiry and identify likely difficulties."
- "Plan a term that bridges blocks, pseudocode and Python; show readiness checks and teacher preparation."

Treat invocation examples as task patterns, not fixed output templates.
