# Default school lesson plan format

Use this reusable format specification for future lesson plans. It is derived from the teacher-supplied 20241014_lessonplan.docx, inspected structurally and visually. Preserve the layout rather than the sample lesson’s content. Do not depend on the original attachment being available; do not bundle its lesson text or personal metadata with the skill.

## Precedence and scope

Use this as the default lesson-plan presentation. A current teacher instruction or supplied template takes precedence. Keep other teachers’ subjects, year levels and local requirements adaptable. Apply this format to lesson plans, not automatically to worksheets, slides or curriculum maps. Retain all the skill’s pedagogical, platform and assessed-task requirements.

## Page and typography

- Use A4 portrait, approximately 210 by 297 mm, with 0.5-inch (12.7 mm) margins on every side.
- Use Times New Roman, 12 pt body text, black. Use Courier New for code/pseudocode; preserve indentation.
- Centre and bold the title: “[Class] [Subject] - Lesson Plan”. Replace placeholders with supplied context; do not carry over the reference’s S4 class.
- Keep a simple monochrome appearance, thin black table borders and light-grey label/header shading. Do not add a cover page, coloured banners, decorative rules or repeated introductory prose.
- Prefer a compact one-page overview when content fits legibly. Continue to a second page when necessary; never force an arbitrary page count by reducing readability or omitting assessment.

## Table 1 Lesson information

Use a four-column grid with approximate column proportions 20%, 23%, 14%, 43%. Centre/bold/shade the label cells and left-align the values. Keep these rows in order:

| Row | First label | Value area | Additional cells |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Date: | [date] | Time: followed by [start/end or duration] |
| 2 | Class: | [class/form] | Venue: followed by [venue] |
| 3 | Teacher: | [teacher] | Merge columns 2 through 4. |
| 4 | Chapter: | [chapter] | Merge columns 2 through 4. |
| 5 | Topic: | [topic] | Merge columns 2 through 4. |
| 6 | Prior knowledge: | [prerequisites] | Merge columns 2 through 4. |
| 7 | Learning objectives: | “Students will be able to:” and observable objectives | Merge columns 2 through 4. |

Rows 1–2 have alternating label/value/label/value cells. Rows 3–7 have one label and a merged three-column value area. Wrap “Learning objectives:” in the label cell if needed. Include objective IDs in the list when mapping evidence across resources.

Use blank values or clear placeholders for an unknown date, venue, teacher or textbook chapter. If only duration is known, write “40 minutes” or the provided duration under Time rather than inventing a clock time. Do not reuse the reference’s date, room, textbook chapter, page references or lesson content.

## Table 2 Lesson outline

After a modest gap, write “Lesson outline:” and add a table with exactly these columns:

| Time | Content | Activity | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| [duration] | [stage, concept and teacher explanation/questions] | [student action and completed task/output] | [resources, platform, objective/evidence checks and support] |

Use approximate column proportions 11%, 50%, 22%, 17%. Centre/bold/shade the header row. Keep body cells left-aligned and the Time column readable. Repeat column headings on continuation pages. Allow row heights to expand; avoid splitting a short row across pages.

Write durations such as “5 mins” in Time; their sum must equal the lesson duration. Do not copy the reference’s exact seven teaching rows. Adapt row count to the lesson and show the five-stage default through Content entries: instruction, example/demo, guided practice/application, evaluation/feedback/improvement, conclusion. Repeat or subdivide stages when useful.

- **Content:** state the teaching focus, essential explanation, demonstration, questions and relevant conceptual steps. Use concise bullets or numbered subpoints within cells.
- **Activity:** state what students actually do, meaningful decisions, task IDs, and the minimum finished product/response. Include at least one task that can be completed and assessed in this lesson. Make worksheet activities traceable by ID.
- **Remarks:** name the recommended platform and required resources; add objective-linked checks, success criteria, scaffold/fallback cues and verified resource references. Do not invent book page numbers. State platform fit and setup briefly here or in the notes below.

## Preserve the additional teaching requirements

Keep the main overview concise. If details cannot fit sensibly in Activity/Remarks, append a short “Teacher notes” section after the outline or on a continuation page covering:
- Platform rationale, preparation, access/connectivity, saving/submission and fallback.
- Core task success criteria, expected evidence and individual assessment for pair/AI work.
- Likely difficulties and responses, differentiation, and relevant theoretical rationale.

These may be combined rather than repeated in separate long report sections. Do not remove required information merely because the original reference had empty Activity or Remarks cells. Keep those cells purposeful in new plans. Check that the completed core task and an individual check together provide evidence for every stated objective; distinguish completion from demonstrated understanding.

## Final format checks

Verify metadata row order and merges; four outline columns and their exact names; timing totals; readable table widths; grey shading; appropriate font use; no inherited sample identifiers; no clipping, split labels or orphaned rows. Render authored Word plans and inspect every page with the documents workflow. Retain editable text and tables.
