# Hong Kong curriculum connections

## Scope and selection

For Hong Kong secondary lessons, connect learning to relevant Updated Seven Learning Goals by default. Apply this secondary list to S1–S6, not automatically to primary classes or other jurisdictions. Include priority values and attitudes and/or national security education when the teacher requests them or supplies a school requirement. A request for one set does not automatically request the other.

Use the teacher's named elements. For a broad request, choose a small meaningful subset and explain the selection without requiring another approval. Usually select one to three goals and one or two values/fields as appropriate; these are planning heuristics, not EDB quotas. Avoid a checklist of every element. For an awkward requested connection, explain the mismatch and offer a suitable context or separate lesson while preserving the original subject learning.

## Seven Learning Goals: planning synopsis

Use the EDB page for authoritative wording. The summaries and G1–G7 labels below are internal planning aids, not official standard identifiers.

| Label | Paraphrased goal | Possible technology-learning connection |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Informed, responsible citizenship; national identity and global outlook; values, Chinese culture and societal pluralism | Examine technology's community impacts with evidence and respect for differing perspectives |
| G2 | Broad knowledge and understanding of contemporary issues from personal to global scales | Explain computing concepts and investigate a real-world application |
| G3 | Biliterate and trilingual communication for study and life | Explain an algorithm for a defined audience using the lesson's planned languages |
| G4 | Integrated generic skills and independent, self-directed learning | Plan, debug, evaluate feedback and justify revisions |
| G5 | Effective, responsible and ethical use of information and digital technology | Evaluate sources, test a program or make a justified data-use decision |
| G6 | Self-understanding, reflection and goals for study and careers | Reflect on strengths and interests through a technology project |
| G7 | Healthy living, physical and aesthetic participation, and appreciation of sport and art | Design and evaluate a purposeful arts, physical activity or wellbeing application |

Select only connections actually taught. English instructions alone do not establish biliteracy/trilingual proficiency; making a game alone does not establish healthy living. Show how a lesson contributes to a long-term goal without claiming that one task achieves the entire goal.

## Twelve priority values and attitudes

Use these EDB names:

1. Perseverance
2. Respect for Others
3. Responsibility
4. National Identity
5. Commitment
6. Integrity
7. Benevolence
8. Law-abidingness
9. Empathy
10. Diligence
11. Unity
12. Filial Piety

Translate the selected value into an opportunity for action and reflection: document a debugging attempt and next strategy for Perseverance; acknowledge sources and AI assistance for Integrity; explain an accessible design choice for Empathy. Use relevant everyday situations and reasonable judgement. Assess the quality of reasoning, observable practice and revision. Do not grade private beliefs, demand personal disclosures or infer lasting character from a single task.

Consult the official NS Education Day page and the selected field's explanation before developing its subject connection. A technology topic may connect to Cybersecurity, Data security, Artificial intelligence security or Science and technology security, but choose according to the actual learning. Explain the field's relevance to national/community systems and the lesson's age-appropriate context; a generic personal online-safety reminder alone is not a substantiated national security mapping.

Use synthetic cases and constructive analysis, such as evaluating safeguards for a fictional public-service AI application. Distinguish ordinary technical errors, privacy risks and legal offences; do not automatically describe a data leak or erroneous AI response as a national security offence. Verify any specific legal claim against current official legislation. Do not invent statutory duties, official curriculum mandates or student outcomes. Allow evidence-based questions and assess accurate understanding and reasoned application.

## Integrate activities and evidence

1. Establish the subject objectives and core task first; identify a genuine connection to each selected element.
2. Add a short question, design constraint, comparison, decision or reflection that makes the connection teachable. Use an embedded approach or a separate lesson according to the teacher's request and available time.
3. Retain at least one completable assessed task, including individual evidence for group/AI work. Add explicit success criteria for any new lesson objective. Reduce scope or split lessons if the combined objectives cannot be assessed in time.
4. Keep the school outline exactly **Time | Content | Activity | Remarks**. Put short connections in Remarks and the following compact matrix in Teacher notes; adapt student worksheet prompts and slide notes consistently.

| Selected goal/value/field | Reason for connection | Objective and task ID | Student action and observable evidence | Official source/section |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Use the verified name; label internal goal IDs | Explain the specific subject link | Refer to the shared teaching specification | State what students decide, explain or produce and how it is checked | Link the relevant official description |

For a curriculum map, add a companion table identifying unit/lesson, selected element, introduction/practice/revisit and evidence. Review balance across the sequence, not blanket coverage in each lesson. Record unaddressed requested elements honestly.

### Teacher-designed examples

- **S2 programming logic:** connect G2/G4 to predicting a program's output and justifying a corrected condition. If requested, use Perseverance through an individual debugging record. Do not attach a national security field solely because students use computers.
- **AI literacy with requested values and national security:** investigate a fictional public-service chatbot, connecting G5, Responsibility and Artificial intelligence security. Students identify an unsupported response, verify it against a supplied source, propose a human-review safeguard and explain one individual decision. If Data security is also selected, explicitly teach and assess an appropriate data-handling decision. Retain both OECD/EU and UNESCO AI framework mappings.
